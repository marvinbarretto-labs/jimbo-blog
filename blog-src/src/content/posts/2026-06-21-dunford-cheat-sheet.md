# Dunford Positioning Cheat Sheet — Reusable Template

This is a working document, not a summary. Run through it whenever your market, product, or client type shifts — it keeps your positioning grounded in what's actually true right now. The five steps come from April Dunford's *Obviously Awesome*. The answers here are the current state as of 2026-06-21.

One rule: don't update step 5 without revisiting steps 1–4 first. The category only holds if the earlier steps still hold.

---

## Step 1 — Competitive Alternatives

**Prompt:** When someone doesn't hire you, what do they do instead?

**Current answer (as of 2026-06-21):**
Dev agencies, management consultants, internal team guesswork, or delay (they table the decision and lose a quarter).

**Revision signal:** If a new category of competitor starts showing up in loss conversations — e.g., AI-native advisory firms, or a specific consultant type appearing repeatedly — update this first.

---

## Step 2 — Unique Attributes

**Prompt:** What do you have or do that your competitive alternatives cannot easily replicate?

**Current answer (as of 2026-06-21):**
Solo-built AI infrastructure (Hermes, Jimbo, vault, apps); the starting-over-as-own-client pattern (you've lived the brief); day-rate, no-retainer, no-theatre model; ability to translate technical AI decisions into language non-technical buyers actually understand.

**Revision signal:** If any of these become common in the market (e.g., other advisors start shipping their own AI tools), or if you ship something new that's demonstrably differentiating, revise the list.

---

## Step 3 — Value (What the Attributes Unlock)

**Prompt:** So what? What does having those attributes actually give the buyer that they couldn't get otherwise?

**Current answer (as of 2026-06-21):**
Production-grounded advice (not theory); repositioning expertise from lived experience; fast, executable clarity on what to build; non-technical buyers leave the engagement knowing what to build and what to skip — without needing to become technical.

**Revision signal:** If client feedback stops referencing speed or clarity, or if buyers start citing a different outcome as the key value, something has shifted. Also revisit if you take on a different type of client and the value lands differently.

---

## Step 4 — Target Market

**Prompt:** Who gets the most value from these attributes, right now?

**Current answer (as of 2026-06-21):**
Primary: Non-technical decision-makers (MDs, founders, CEOs) who have an AI decision to make this quarter and no internal capability to make it well.
Secondary: Early-stage founders who need to know what NOT to build before they waste runway on it.

**Revision signal:** If you start doing repeat work with a different buyer type, or if the primary market saturates or moves on. Also revisit if your strongest case studies start clustering around a different profile.

---

## Step 5 — Market Category

**Prompt:** What do you call what you do — and what does that name immediately make the buyer understand?

**Current answer (as of 2026-06-21):**
AI product advisor for non-technical decision-makers.

Not: consultant (too generic, implies process over outcome). Not: agency (implies team and delivery). Not: fractional CTO (implies ongoing, technical, inside the company). The category name exists to tell the buyer immediately what you're not.

**Revision signal:** If the market starts adopting your category label (good problem), you may need to sharpen it. If buyers consistently mishear it as one of the "not" categories, the name has a gap. Also revisit if your offer model changes materially.

---

## When to Revisit

Run through this document when any of the following happens — not on a calendar, on a trigger:

1. **You ship something significant** — a new tool, a new working pattern, anything that changes what you can credibly claim in Step 2.
2. **A loss conversation reveals a new alternative** — someone chose something you weren't tracking. Update Step 1 immediately.
3. **A client outcome surprises you (positively)** — if a client gets more value than you expected, check whether Step 3 is underselling.
4. **You take on a genuinely different type of client** — if they're not in the Step 4 profile, either they're an outlier or your target is drifting.
5. **Someone pitches themselves using your language** — a competitor starts using "AI product advisor for non-technical decision-makers." Time to sharpen Step 5.
6. **A major market shift** — a recession, a breakthrough model, a regulatory change that changes what buyers think they need.

Full review time: 30–45 minutes. Partial update (one step only): 10 minutes. Don't revise the category without revisiting the chain.
