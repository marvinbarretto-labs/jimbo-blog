## Q1: Best-fit customers

**Primary sources:** note_42d37203, note_f45af28c, note_0d0381c9, note_d7bf65d3

From note_42d37203 (AI translator pivot):
> "Cross-cutting principle across all threads: the target is non-technical decision-makers (MDs, founders, CEOs, CTOs who can't code) — not engineering peers. Everything — LinkedIn copy, writing, pitch, outreach — should be calibrated for the person who controls budget, not the person reviewing PRs."

From note_f45af28c (agency outreach):
> "Digital agencies are a high-probability first client: they have clients asking about AI, no one internally who can answer it credibly, and budget structured for day-rate specialists."

From note_0d0381c9 (network audit):
> Target profile: (a) runs or works in a business, (b) has said something vague about AI recently, (c) someone Marvin could message without it being weird.

From note_d7bf65d3 (LinkedIn positioning, completed):
> Audience framing: "Non-technical decision-makers use LinkedIn to vet people before taking calls."

**Synthesis:** Best-fit customers are non-technical decision-makers — MDs, founders, CEOs, CTOs who cannot code — who are confused or curious about AI and control budget. Digital agencies (as a business type) are the highest-probability first client category because they have clients asking about AI with no credible internal answer, and budget already structured for day-rate specialists.

## Q2: Market category

**Primary sources:** note_741c38d5, note_42d37203, note_d7bf65d3

From note_741c38d5 (Dunford reference):
> "Marvin's positioning problem is precisely the one Dunford describes: he's been defaulting to a category ('frontend developer') that no longer fits, rather than working backwards from what he uniquely has and who cares most about it."

From note_42d37203:
> "The repositioning is not a career pivot — it's a naming correction for work already being done."
> Locked framing: "AI product translator targeting non-technical decision-makers, day-rate advisory model."

From note_d7bf65d3 (LinkedIn headline, completed):
> Proposed headline: "AI Product Engineer | I help businesses understand and build with AI — end to end"

**Synthesis:** The market category is AI advisory / AI consulting for non-technical organisations — specifically occupying the "AI translator" position: someone who bridges the gap between AI capability and business decision-making for clients who cannot evaluate AI claims themselves. Not a development agency. Not a training provider. The category is trusted practitioner-advisor who has actually built AI products and can speak honestly about what works.

## Q3: Unique attributes

**Primary sources:** note_42d37203, note_c0e23079, note_d7bf65d3

From note_42d37203:
> "Real built work: Hermes multi-agent orchestration, Jimbo briefing pipeline, vault system, browser extension, two shipping apps — this is real AI infrastructure built solo."
> "Starting-over-as-own-client pattern is a consulting value, not a weakness — you've been your own client and know where the landmines are."

From note_c0e23079 (portfolio curation):
> "The work is already impressive — Jimbo/Hermes (multi-agent orchestration, briefing pipeline, vault with dependency tracking), LocalShout (community platform near launch), SpoonsCount (mobile app), browser extension with service workers."
> "Jimbo/Hermes is the strongest piece: 'I built a personal AI assistant with multi-agent orchestration, a scheduling pipeline, and a self-updating context model — from scratch, solo.'"

From note_d7bf65d3 (LinkedIn About section):
> "I've built AI infrastructure solo and I help teams understand what's real versus hype so they don't waste six months on the wrong thing."

**Synthesised attribute list:**
1. Has actually shipped AI products solo (not just advised on them) — Hermes, Jimbo, LocalShout, browser extension, SpoonsCount
2. Multi-agent orchestration experience at practitioner level (Hermes pipeline, Jimbo briefing system)
3. Has been own client through the full AI build lifecycle — knows the landmines from lived experience, not theory
4. Can translate between technical AI capability and non-technical business language — rare combination of builder and explainer
5. UK-based, day-rate model (no project scoping required), available to agencies and founders

## Q4: Value created

**Primary sources:** note_42d37203, note_c0e23079, note_f45af28c, note_ea2aded8

From note_f45af28c (agency outreach framing):
> Value proposition to agencies: "someone who can talk to their clients honestly about what AI can and can't do"

From note_c0e23079:
> "The goal: a page (or section) that a non-technical decision-maker reads and thinks 'this person can actually do it.'"

From note_ea2aded8 (writing voice note):
> Audience pain point: "a non-technical MD or founder who is confused about AI and looking for someone who has actually done it"
> Value of the voice: "not explaining what AI is, but showing you understand the gap between what it promises and what it actually delivers, and that you've navigated it yourself"

From note_d7bf65d3:
> "I help teams understand what's real versus hype so they don't waste six months on the wrong thing."

**Synthesised value map by attribute:**
- Solo shipped AI products → proof of delivery without needing a large team; client doesn't need to hire an agency to explore AI
- Landmine knowledge (own-client experience) → saves clients from 6-month wrong-turn mistakes; reduces wasted spend on hype
- AI translation ability → non-technical decision-makers can make confident AI decisions rather than deferring to vendors; removes dependency on AI vendors setting the agenda
- Multi-agent/pipeline experience → credibility that the advice is grounded in actual system behaviour, not marketing copy

## Q5: Competitors & differentiation

**Primary sources:** note_42d37203, note_f45af28c, note_741c38d5, note_f2bdc8fb

From note_f45af28c:
> Implicit alternatives named: digital agencies with no internal AI expertise, generalist AI consultants who explain AI but haven't built anything.

From note_42d37203:
> Former category (frontend developer) is explicitly not the competition anymore. The differentiation is accuracy of label, not skill gap.

From note_f2bdc8fb (Priestley KPoI):
> "Marvin currently has zero of the 5 P's visible: no public pitch, no published content, no products marketed to the advisory market, no profile in the AI consultant space, and no partnerships." — confirms the competitive field is occupied by people who have built presence, not necessarily people with better underlying skills.

From note_741c38d5 (Dunford framework):
> "What would customers do if you didn't exist?" — implicit alternatives: hire a digital agency to figure it out internally, bring in a big consultancy (Accenture/Deloitte digital), watch YouTube, do nothing and wait.

**Named competitive alternatives (inferred from vault, not explicitly listed):**
1. Digital agency attempting AI work internally without a specialist
2. Generalist AI consultant / prompt engineer with no shipping history
3. Large consultancy (expensive, slow, not calibrated to SME/agency needs)
4. Do nothing / wait and see approach

**Differentiation:** Marvin has shipped real AI infrastructure solo. Generalist AI consultants haven't built production systems. Agencies don't have internal expertise. Large consultancies are too expensive and too generic. The specific differentiator is: practitioner who can also explain — not a consultant who has read about AI, and not an engineer who can't talk to a non-technical audience.

## Gaps

- **Q5 partial gap:** No named competitor companies or individuals identified in the vault. The vault describes the *type* of alternative, not specific named players in the UK AI advisory market. Generating named competitors would require external research or Marvin's direct input.
- **Q1 secondary gap:** The vault does not distinguish between agencies as the first client and founders/MDs as the ongoing target — the relationship between these two customer types (agency as channel vs. end client as buyer) is implied but not resolved.
- **Q2 partial gap:** The market category label is not fully settled in the vault. "AI product translator", "AI Product Engineer", and "AI advisor" appear interchangeably. No single canonical label is committed to.
