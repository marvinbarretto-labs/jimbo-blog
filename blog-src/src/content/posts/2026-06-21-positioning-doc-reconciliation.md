## Living Positioning Doc — One-Pager

### 1. Who are your best-fit customers?

Non-technical decision-makers with budget authority and an AI decision they can't avoid this quarter. MDs, founders, CEOs, and CTOs who run business units — not engineering teams. They're not looking to understand AI. They need to make a call, and they need to make it soon.

Secondary: early-stage founders building their first AI-adjacent product who need to know what NOT to build before they burn six months on the wrong thing.

The qualifier is urgency. If they can kick the decision to next quarter, they will. Best-fit clients are the ones who can't.

---

### 2. What market category are you competing in?

**AI product advisor for non-technical decision-makers.**

Deliberately not: consultant, agency, fractional CTO.

"AI consultant" is a race to the bottom against 50,000 ChatGPT accounts. Narrow is defensible. The category is specific enough to be meaningful and broad enough to move.

---

### 3. What are your unique attributes?

- **Real AI infrastructure built solo** — Hermes, Jimbo, a personal vault, a browser extension, two apps. Not whiteboard consulting. The advice comes from someone who's hit real walls, not someone who's read about them.
- **The starting-over pattern** — Marvin has been his own client through a mid-career repositioning. He knows the landmines from the inside.
- **Day-rate model, no scoping theatre** — one day, one outcome. No six-week discovery phase, no 40-slide deck, no procurement overhead.
- **Non-technical to technical translation** — maps the MD's business problem to the right AI architecture in language that doesn't require a computer science degree to follow.

---

### 4. What value do those attributes create for your customers?

| Attribute | Client outcome |
|---|---|
| Real built infrastructure | Advice from someone who's hit real walls — pattern recognition, not theory |
| Starting-over pattern | Recognises the repositioning moment from the inside — doesn't flinch at the messy middle |
| Day-rate model | Fast, executable clarity with no procurement overhead and nothing to un-scope |
| Non-technical translation | Non-technical buyers walk away knowing what to build, what to skip, and what to ask their dev team |

---

### 5. Who are your competitors and why are you different?

**Dev agencies** — two weeks of discovery, six weeks building the wrong thing. You get output but not necessarily the right output, and the clock starts from day one of a long runway.

**Management consultants** — 40-slide deck, zero executable output. Frameworks without decisions.

**Internal team "figuring out AI"** — never shipped an AI product. Good intentions, no track record.

**Delay** — the most common competitor. The meeting gets rescheduled, the decision drifts to next quarter, the opportunity window closes.

The difference: one day, someone who has built the thing, no theatre. The client leaves with a decision, not a deck.
