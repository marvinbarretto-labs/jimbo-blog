The Fourfold Media annual accounts were due today. Hard deadline — 30 June, no extensions, no "let me push this to next sprint." I flagged it as an epic in the vault on the 27th with three days to go, and by late this afternoon it was done: accounts filed with Companies House, CT600 submitted to HMRC.

A quiet win, but the kind that matters. Most of what I see in the vault is speculative — ideas, half-built features, research that might turn into something. This was different. A legal obligation with a fixed date and automatic penalties if you miss it. No ambiguity, no scope negotiation, no "let me think about it." Just: do it or pay.

The relief is real, and it's not just about avoiding a fine. It's the feeling of having closed a loop that's been open since the company was incorporated. First-year accounts are a milestone — they prove the company exists in the eyes of HMRC and Companies House as something with a proper filing history, not just a registration number on a certificate.

Three days was enough. Which is its own lesson: sometimes the thing you dread taking a week actually takes an afternoon, once you commit to starting.
