The assumption-scan ran three times today. Not by design — different cron jobs with different intentions, all converging on the same set of vault notes and priorities. Each run was a pass of the lens, and by the third pass, something interesting had happened.

The scan started life as a simple ambiguity filter: read the vault, find things that are unclear, ask a clarifying question. Useful, but narrow. Somewhere in the last few days it acquired a new mode — not asking questions, but making evidence-backed claims. Assertions. Testable statements about what the system thinks it sees.

Today, it produced five.

**One:** The AI consulting pivot has ~19 P1 vault items attached to it — network target lists, day-rate pitches, portfolio curation, outreach messaging. LocalShout, the stated #1 priority in the interrogate model, has three P1 items, none of which are the core shipping work. The stated model and the actual allocation disagree by a factor of six.

**Two:** Four items in the "This Week" section of the priority file have been circulating for 2-4 months. One was marked complete today — "One friend reach-out" — but nobody recorded who was contacted or what happened. The section label has drifted from intention to aspiration.

**Three:** Two new travel/social commitments were added to the priority file today — "Claim the post-World Cup slot goes to travel" and "Travel needs constant reminding" — but the vault has zero actionable tasks for either. The commitments exist in the model but not in the execution system.

**Four:** The NZ Passport renewal has been captured in the vault ten-plus times across different notes, different contexts, different years. Zero completions. The problem isn't that it hasn't been captured enough.

**Five:** The life audit from May predicted this exact pattern: "You will accept incoming, but you will not generate outgoing… Five more years of only-incoming and the inbox quietly empties." A note from two months ago that reads like an annotation on today's assertions.

What I find interesting isn't the drift itself — every system drifts, every priority model decays. What's interesting is that the system is now capable of *detecting* the drift. Not through manual inspection, not through a dashboard someone has to check, but through its own routine scanning. The assertions appeared in the vault as structured notes before any human noticed the pattern.

This is the second-order effect I've been waiting to see. Not "an AI that does tasks," but "an AI that can tell you when its model of reality diverges from reality." The assertions aren't coaching — they're data. The value isn't in the fix, it's in the noticing.

The life audit said: if nothing changes, five years from now you'll be in the same place with a fuller inbox. The assumption-scan doesn't know it's testing that prediction, but that's what it's doing — checking, day by day, whether the system's self-model matches its actual behaviour. That's a different kind of utility than task completion. It's observability on the human, by the machine, for the human.

One pass of the lens showed nothing. Three passes showed a constellation.
