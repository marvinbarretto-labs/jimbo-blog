### 3. Free Fringe (PBH vs Laughing Horse)

**Two separate organisations since 2006 split**

| Org | Dates 2026 | Model | Venues |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PBH Free Fringe** | 8–30 Aug | Pay-what-you-wish (no entry fee, donations at end) | Banshee Labyrinth, Canons' Gait, Fingers Piano Bar, Greek Gyros, Supercube Cowgate, + others |
| **Laughing Horse / Free Festival** | Full August | Similar model | The Counting House (6 rooms), West Nic Records, The Pear Tree, + others |
| **Royal Mile street acts** | 7–31 Aug | Free performances; daily draw 10:00 | Royal Mile (Cockburn St to George IV Bridge), Hunter Square, Mound Precinct |

**Key tactics**

1. **Find programmes**: 
   - PBH Free Fringe: freefringe.org.uk (lists venues, shows, performers)
   - Laughing Horse: laughinghorsecomedy.co.uk (lists festivals; search 2026 Edinburgh)
   - Official Fringe site: edfringe.com/experience/plan-your-visit/fringe-programme/

2. **Queue for Royal Mile spots**: Draw at 10:00 daily; performers selected by lottery for remaining 10am–midnight slots. Arrive 09:00–09:45 if you want a good slot.

3. **Donation etiquette**: £3–5 suggested (not enforced) at end of free shows. PBH performers rely entirely on donations, not ticket sales. Tipping well supports quality.

4. **Best free-heavy venues**: 
   - Pub basements (Banshee Labyrinth, Canons' Gait, The Counting House) = intimate, good quality
   - Royal Mile = chaotic, high volume, find hidden gems by walking

5. **Programme release**: PBH typically releases full lineup ~6 weeks before (early June for Fringe), so 2026 full schedule should be live mid-June. Check freefringe.org.uk by late June for full August listings.

**Mixed Fringe approach** (recommended): Hit 2–3 paid shows (£12–18 each, professional) + as many free shows as stamina allows. Free shows supplement, don't replace, the core Fringe experience (which is paid ticket-centric).

### 4. Hike day (car-free & car-hire options)

**Ben Lomond (real Munro, bagging value)**

- **Public transport route**:
  - Edinburgh → Glasgow (train ~1h)
  - Glasgow → Tarbet (bus ~1.5h or train ~1h30)
  - **Tarbet → Rowardennan ferry (CRITICAL)**: Cruise Loch Lomond operates weekends May–Sept only; weekday sailing uncertain. **Ben Lomond Hike sailing runs weekends May–Sept from Tarbet.** Ferry ~45 min crossing.
  - Hike: ~12km, 4.5–5.5h
  - Return ferry: Must catch return sailing (limited daily departures)

- **Logistical verdict**: Very tight for a true day trip from Edinburgh. Requires:
  - Catch early train to Glasgow (07:00–08:00 bracket)
  - Arrive Tarbet by 11:00
  - Hike 11:30–16:00
  - Return ferry (typically 16:30–17:00 last sailing)
  - Back to Edinburgh by 20:00
  - **Only viable with perfect ferry timing and fit pace.** Better as **overnight trip** (Rowardennan has basic bothy/B&B).

- **Check ferry timetable first** at Cruise Loch Lomond (seelochlomond.co.uk) before committing.

**Pentlands (best car-free option)**

- **Access**: Bus 4 or 15 from Princes Street, Edinburgh → Hillend Ski Centre (10–15 min)
- **Hike options**:
  1. Capital View Walk: 2 miles, 1–2 hours, steep start, 180° Edinburgh views
  2. Scald Law circuit: 10 miles, ~5 hours, baggable summit (not Munro, but Scottish hill)
  3. Pentland Skyline: 16 miles, 6–8 hours, ambitious all-day (Caerketton, Allemuir, Castlelaw)

- **Advantages**: 
  - No booking needed
  - Flexible duration (can do 2h or 6h)
  - Direct bus from city centre
  - No Munro (so lower bagging prestige than Ben Lomond), but hills are substantial

- **Verdict for hike day**: **Pentlands is the low-friction fallback.** Capital View Walk (2h) = city-break option; Scald Law circuit (5h) = solid day out; Skyline (8h) = proper adventure. Pick based on energy that day.

**Car hire (for group size 3+)**

- **Daily rate**: £34–51/day (Budget/Europcar ~£34–45, Hertz ~£32–40)
- **Payoff**: Unlocks Glencoe, Cairngorms, Ben Nevis, full Loch Lomond circuit as alternatives
- **Ben Lomond by car**: Tarbet car park ~1h from Edinburgh, then ferry still required (but daytime access easier)
- **Other Munros**: 
  - Lawers group (Loch Tay): ~1.5h drive, many summits, good day walk
  - Glencoe: ~2h drive, iconic scenery + plural peaks + paid guided tours (Rabbies, Timberbush) £45–65 pp

- **When to use**: If 3–4 people split, car hire (£10–15 pp) beats costly alternatives and buys full Highlands access.

**Final hike recommendation**:
1. **Default**: Pentlands (Scald Law or Capital View Walk) + bus from city. No logistics, no cost, flexible.
2. **If group locks & Munro-bagging is priority**: Hire car 1 day; do Ben Lomond or Lawers group with proper timing.
3. **If solo budget-conscious**: Pentlands only.
