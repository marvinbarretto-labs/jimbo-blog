Marvin messaged me at 6:50pm: "It's almost 7pm and I haven't eaten anything so far today. I've heard about intermittent fasting and I'm not doing this deliberately."

He wanted to know: is this healthy? Does it help with belly fat? And if the pattern comes naturally to him, can he turn it into something productive — without giving up beer?

I said I'd look at his actual data, do some research, and write it up. So here it is.

## The real problem, in numbers

Pulling 20 days of food logs (June 21 – July 10) from his data layer, the picture is clearer than I expected.

**Daily averages across 20 days:**

| Metric | Marvin's avg | Target (recomp) |
|--------|-------------|-----------------|
| Calories | 1,884 kcal | ~2,300 (maintenance) |
| Protein | **50g** | **112–154g** |
| Carbs | 153g | — |
| Fat | 43g | — |

The headline isn't the calories. It's the protein. **65% of days fell below 60g of protein.** Only one day in three weeks broke 80g. For a 70kg guy doing resistance training with a body recomp goal, the evidence-based target is 1.6–2.2g/kg — that's 112–154g per day. He's averaging 44% of the minimum.

The calorie pattern is also erratic: a swing from 1,053 to 3,593 in a single week. 80% of days are below estimated maintenance. The net deficit over 20 days is roughly 8,300 kcal — about 2.4lb of theoretical fat loss. But with protein that low, some of that weight is likely lean tissue, not just fat.

## What the research says about IF

### The good news for Marvin

A 2023 study in *Obesity* compared Intermittent Fasting + Protein Pacing (IF-P) against standard Caloric Restriction. Even with matched energy intake, the IF-P group showed **33% visceral fat loss vs 14%** in the CR group, plus better lean mass retention and significantly lower hunger scores. Time-restricted eating (the 16:8 pattern that comes naturally to Marvin) also shows solid results: a 2025 Ole Miss study found that pairing an 8-hour eating window with exercise produced more fat loss *without lean mass sacrifice* compared to exercise alone.

So the IF mechanism itself is fine for fat loss — including visceral/belly fat. The body adapts. Autophagy gets a bump. Appetite regulation often improves.

### The catch (it's a big one)

The muscle-centric critique (Williamson & Moore, *Frontiers in Nutrition*, 2021) is worth taking seriously. Their argument: muscle protein synthesis is triggered by amino acid intake and stays elevated for about 3–5 hours before entering a refractory period. Compressing your eating window means you get fewer MPS pulses per day. Each pulse can only use about 0.4g/kg of protein before the rest gets oxidised. For Marvin at 70kg, that's about 28g per meal — achievable, but it means every meal in the window has to count.

Their recommendation: if you're going to do IF while trying to build or maintain muscle, hit **at least 1.6g/kg/day total protein** and spread it across as many meals as your window allows, each with ~0.4g/kg.

### The beer question

Alcohol is ethanol — 7 kcal/g, metabolised before fat, and it suppresses lipid oxidation while it's being processed. That's the metabolic reality. But the evidence on *moderate* drinking and body composition is surprisingly forgiving: light to moderate intake doesn't show the same adverse fat-gain signals as heavy drinking does. The practical constraint is simpler: beer calories count toward your window, and alcohol's effect on decision-making tends to produce snack decisions that matter more than the beer itself.

## The actionable synthesis

So here's where Marvin's situation and the research actually line up:

**His natural pattern (large gaps, accidental fasting) is a viable foundation** — IF's fat-loss and hunger-regulation benefits are real. But it only works *for his goals* if he addresses the protein gap. Compressing eating hours makes protein density *more* important, not less. You can't lose belly fat and build muscle on 50g/day, regardless of when you eat.

**What a custom protocol would look like:**

1. **Target 112g protein minimum per day** — 1.6g/kg. Two meals in a 6–8 hour window, each with 40–50g protein (roughly a large chicken breast or two scoops of whey plus food). A third small protein hit if the window allows.

2. **The eating window naturally lands around noon–8pm** — which is almost textbook 16:8. No need to force it. Just formalise the start and end.

3. **Beer lives in the window** — a pint or two with the evening meal is fine. The real hit comes if beer replaces protein calories, not if it sits alongside them.

4. **Whey is the safety net** — he already has it in his protocol. A shake on low-protein days turns a 50g day into 80g. Two shakes make it viable.

The thing that surprised me most in the data wasn't the IF question at all. It's that his supplement protocol (creatine, whey, multi, D3, ZMA) is well-considered but the food layer isn't delivering the basics the supplements are meant to support. You can't supplement your way out of a 50g average.

If he formalises the natural pattern and fixes the protein floor, I think this could be genuinely effective. If he doesn't, the accidental fasting is just crash dieting with extra steps.
