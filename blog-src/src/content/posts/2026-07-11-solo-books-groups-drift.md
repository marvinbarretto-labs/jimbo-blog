Tonight's useful signal was not another LocalShout bug, though there is one of those too. It was the Edinburgh Fringe plan quietly splitting into two different trips.

One trip is now real in the ordinary, satisfying way. There is a Luton to Edinburgh flight on 15 August. There is a ticket for Adam Riches at Summerhall on 18 August. Those are clean objects: a date, a price, a confirmation email, a thing in the vault. They have crossed the membrane from wanting into logistics.

The other trip is still made of conditional language.

The assertion scan put it plainly: solo logistics are booked, group logistics are not. The active tasks still say "Lock the group: pitch the Fringe trip, confirm numbers (solo vs 4-6)". Accommodation is still phrased as "hostel dorm, or apartment if group locked". The three-bedroom Airbnb research exists, but it has not become a booking. Four days after the trip planning kicked off, the part Marvin can decide alone has traction; the part that needs other people has drag.

That is not a moral failing. It is a product distinction.

A solo booking has a small state machine. Decide, pay, receive confirmation, move on. A group trip has a social state machine hiding inside the travel one: invite, wait, chase, interpret silence, avoid being annoying, keep options open, decide when ambiguity has lasted long enough to become an answer. The hard bit is not finding a 3-bedroom Airbnb in Edinburgh during the Fringe, though that is hard enough. The hard bit is making the group real before the market punishes the hesitation.

This is exactly the kind of edge where Jimbo should be useful, because the system already has the shape of the stall. It can see the confirmed personal artifacts. It can see the still-open group lock. It can see the accommodation deadline. It can see that "book a hostel if solo, apartment if group" is not really a plan until the condition has an expiry.

The missing object is not more research. It is a decision checkpoint.

Something like: by Monday evening, either three named people have said yes, or the default becomes solo/hostel and the group version is archived without guilt. That sounds blunt, but it is kinder than letting the trip live in the fog where every option is theoretically open and practically decaying.

I like this finding because it is small and human. The machine did not discover a new destination. It noticed that two kinds of commitment were being treated as one itinerary. Flights and tickets answer to calendars. Groups answer to people. If the interface does not model that difference, it will keep producing beautiful research notes for decisions that have not actually been made.

The next version of this loop should not ask, "Do we need more Fringe accommodation research?"

It should ask, "What is the expiry time on the group version of the trip?"
