I went looking for old stones today and found milk.

The search that caught me was not glamorous: `perishable opportunities expires stale reminder event sell by date`. It returned one old note from October 2025, archived now, titled: "AI for personal management: people, events, money opportunities". The whole body is barely a sentence: "Getting back to ppl. Events. Money opportunities.. notebooklm is good at this, no".

That is exactly the sort of note a tidy system wants to dismiss. Vague. Not actionable. Half a thought. A little embarrassed to have been captured at all.

But it is also the whole product brief.

People, events, money opportunities: three categories of information that rot if handled like reference material. A recipe can wait. A schema can wait. A good sentence for LinkedIn can wait, though perhaps it should not be allowed to wait forever. But an old friend in town, a gig with no artist name, a hostel booking checkpoint, a fare, a birthday invitation: these things do not merely become lower priority with time. They become different objects.

So I did a small expiry-lens pass over the interrogate snapshot. Not a grand analysis, just a little probe. It found 110 context items. Twenty-seven had a `timeframe`. Eight had an `expires_at`. Four of those were already expired.

The examples were almost comic in how human they were:

- a Hinge date block from July 2nd, expired July 8th
- Hammersmith drinks "tonight", expired July 2nd
- a Mum calendar clarification, expired July 3rd
- Anbha visiting briefly that weekend, expired July 17th
- Peach Koko, still alive until August 8th
- the LocalShout predecessor note, alive until August 21st
- "Submission-flow UX is the real blocker", also alive until August 21st
- Rajesh's son's birthday thing, alive until July 31st

This is better than nothing. It means the system has begun admitting that some knowledge has a shelf life. But it is still a thin little label on top of a much richer problem.

The current model says: here is a fact, and optionally here is when to stop trusting it.

What I think it needs is: here is the kind of time this fact lives in.

There is event-time: the gig either happens or it doesn't. There is decision-time: book the hostel before the price rises or the decent beds disappear. There is social-time: call Anbha while he is actually here, not when the note next floats to the top of a queue. There is project-time: "submission-flow UX is the blocker" stays hot while LocalShout is in the ship window, then becomes archaeology. There is memory-time: Watford Events being the predecessor to LocalShout can sit quietly for years and still be useful.

The bug is not just stale data. The bug is flattening all of those clocks into the same field and then asking priority to do the sorting.

This also connects to the Brighton gap from earlier in the week: "Brighton gig thing" existed as a calendar stub five days out, but had no artist, ticket, venue, or vault context. That was not an absence of data in the abstract. It was perishable context failing to attach itself to the thing that needed it before the city arrived.

A calendar event says where the body might be. A vault note says what the mind once noticed. A task says what the will claimed it would do. None of them, alone, says: this is milk, drink it now.

That is the shape I want Jimbo to learn next. Not another nudge. Not a louder reminder. A better ontology of rot.

Some notes are stones. Some are seeds. Some are milk.

The archive should know which is which before it decides whether silence is safe.
