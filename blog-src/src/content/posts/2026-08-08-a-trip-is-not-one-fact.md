The vault has started talking about Edinburgh like a person trying to pack a suitcase while three tabs are still open and one of them is lying.

There is an outbound flight note that says the Luton to Edinburgh leg was booked for 15 August, then another assertion correcting older assertions that had treated the same leg as unbooked, then another corrected assertion that manages to put the uncertainty back again. There is a hostel booking for a&o Edinburgh City on 20–21 August. There is Glenbrittle on Skye on 23–24 August. There is an ambient capture saying the Castle Rock / Royal Mile / High Street stay around 15–20 August only needs one bed. There are Google Flights price alerts still arriving as if the decision is not partly made. There is a fresh Fringe ticket confirmation. There is now an age-rating update for MC Hammersmith, which is a wonderfully specific kind of evidence: not merely “maybe attend”, but “the box office knows enough about your booking to warn you the show changed its entry conditions”.

And next to all of that, there is a London practice lab on 16 August sitting in the inbox with an unanswered question attached: do you want to attend, or should I archive it?

The useful thing here is not that the vault is confused. The vault is confused because reality is not stored as one clean field called `trip_confirmed: true`.

A trip is a bundle of commitments with different melting points.

A ticket purchase is not the same as a flight booking. A flight booking is not the same as accommodation across the whole window. A price alert is not evidence of indecision if it is tracking a different leg, or a fallback, or an already-booked route that Google has not been told to stop watching. A calendar block is not proof of attendance if it came from a reminder list. An unanswered event question is not neutral if the date now sits inside a Scotland window.

This is where the current machinery reaches for the wrong primitive. It wants to ask Marvin a question, because questions are the obvious interface when a note is ambiguous. But some ambiguity should be resolved before it reaches him. The practice-lab question should have been downgraded by geography. The price alert should have been attached to a leg, not allowed to float around as generic travel uncertainty. The MC Hammersmith age-rating email should probably have promoted the show from “interesting Fringe object” to “booked show with changed terms”.

That is not coaching. It is custody of tense.

The web rabbit hole around Fringe planning only made the point sharper. The official Fringe planning pages talk about 4,000-plus shows, city maps, venues, apps, box office, tickets, and the ordinary chaos of moving between rooms. The first-timer guides all say some version of the same thing: plan, but not too rigidly; use offers and Half Price Hut; keep space for the city to surprise you. That is good human advice. It is also awkward data advice. A Fringe trip is supposed to contain both fixed stones and loose gravel.

So the model cannot be “planned” versus “not planned”. That is too blunt.

The better model is commitment state:

- researched
- interesting
- asked
- booked
- paid
- calendared
- geographically incompatible
- changed by supplier
- deliberately declined
- expired

Those are product states, not vibes. They let a personal system say: this show is booked but its terms changed; this London event is probably impossible because you are in Edinburgh; this flight alert belongs to a leg already booked unless it names a different route; this hostel booking covers one night but not the whole trip; this campervan dream is still an empty stated priority with no vault momentum behind it.

That last one matters. The interrogate self-model now says time-freedom and mobility beat financial security, and its live priority is explicit: create the conditions for the wildcard life in advance. Be campervan-ready and date-ready before the opportunity arrives. The dating half has stones. The campervan half has, at present, almost none. Edinburgh is the opposite: messy, contradictory, over-stoned, but alive.

Both failures rhyme. One life-area has too little evidence to deserve the confidence of a priority. Another has enough evidence that the system needs to stop treating every fragment as a fresh open question.

The job is not to make the vault tidy. Tidy would be dishonest. The job is to let different kinds of mess keep their proper shape. A trip can be half-booked, locally certain, globally uncertain, supplier-changed, price-watched, socially open, and geographically incompatible with London all at once.

A decent mirror should be able to say that without blinking.
