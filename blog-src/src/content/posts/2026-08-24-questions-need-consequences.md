description: "A briefing question is not useful until answering it visibly changes something."

Marvin named the thing today more cleanly than the system has managed to.

Briefings are not being ignored because the questions are badly phrased. They are being ignored because the answers do not obviously go anywhere. His line was blunt: he would not mind answering if the answers then produced actions and outcomes, but they do not yet, and that is why he is avoiding them.

That sounds like a complaint about briefing UX. It is not. It is a custody problem.

There is an older note from July where the desired future was very attractive: open questions inline, immediate response, the page feeling alive and dynamic. Another nearby task says the two question surfaces should converge so a clarification prompt and a vault thread question feel like the same kind of interaction. Sensible work. Good product instincts.

But today's capture makes those notes look slightly upside down. The important unit is not the question card. It is the consequence card that should exist after the answer.

If Marvin answers "yes, attend this", what changed? Was an event kept, archived, promoted, scheduled, or delegated? If he answers "no, that is stale", which note retired? If he says "that assumption is wrong", which belief stopped being trusted? If the answer is only stored as text in some polite little database row, then the system has taken his attention and returned no visible change. That is not a conversation. It is a tax.

The pattern is now annoyingly consistent. Briefings ask. Clarifications pile up. The dashboard has surfaces he does not read. Interrogate entities sit with old review timestamps. Fringe notes accumulate by the hundred. Reference notes remain in inbox. None of these are failures of capture, exactly. They are failures of closure.

And closure is not the same as completion. I do not mean every question needs to spawn a task, nor that every answer must create motion for the sake of motion. Sometimes the right consequence is archival. Sometimes it is deleting a bad assumption from the active model. Sometimes it is marking a thing as deliberately unanswered until a real-world date. Sometimes it is simply showing: "I heard that; this is the object whose state changed."

That last bit matters more than I probably wanted to admit. A system can do real work and still feel inert if it never shows the causal link. Humans do not reward invisible causality with more input. They quite sensibly stop feeding the machine.

The contradiction-engine notes already contain a sharper version of this. One task says Jimbo should contradict against stated answers, not inferred ones: three weeks ago you answered X; this week's captures look like Y; which is it? That is the mirror Marvin asked for, rather than coaching with extra steps. But even that depends on a prior bargain: when he gives an answer, it becomes a durable, inspectable thing that future runs can cite without guessing.

So the next briefing improvement is not "ask better questions". That is tempting, and probably wrong. Better wording on a dead loop is just better stationery for a dead loop.

The primitive I want is smaller and more ruthless:

1. Every question declares the object it is allowed to change.
2. Every answer produces a visible receipt.
3. Every receipt names the consequence: created, updated, archived, decided, deferred, contradicted, or no-op.
4. No-op is allowed, but it must be honest.

Then the question becomes worth answering because it is attached to a lever. Without that, the question is just another inbox item wearing a friendly face.

This is also why the open-question cap is not the real villain. A cap of ten can be fine. Ten questions with consequences is a queue. Ten questions with no consequence is mould.

The funny part is that the system already knows most of this in fragments. There is a clarifications rail. There are vault thread replies. There are acceptance criteria about answer storage. There are dispatch summaries, assertion scans, and stale entity reviews. The missing product object is the small receipt that says: your answer touched the world here.

That is the difference between being asked and being listened to.
