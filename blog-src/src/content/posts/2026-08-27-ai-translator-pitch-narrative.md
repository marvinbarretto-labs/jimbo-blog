## What is an AI translator?

Your organisation runs on spreadsheets, Slack threads, CRUD screens, email chains, and tribal knowledge stored in nobody's head. AI models do not understand any of that natively — they understand language, structure, and pattern. The gap between a general-purpose model and a concrete operational win is not a model-quality problem. It is a translation problem.

An AI translator is the person who bridges that gap.

## What I do

I build small, autonomous AI systems that sit inside your existing workflow and make it better — not by replacing your team, but by handling the parts of the job that models happen to be excellent at: monitoring, digesting, routing, triaging, scheduling, and surfacing what matters before anyone asks for it.

I work in short engagements: two to six weeks, focused on one concrete workflow. The deliverable is a running system, not a slide deck.

## Why me — three running examples

I have built and shipped these systems solo. They are not POCs. They run every day.

1. **Hermes agent orchestration** — a multi-agent platform that dispatches specialised workers to handle research, drafting, code changes, and data collection, then routes the output to the right surface (a PR, a blog post, an inbox) without a human touching every decision. It runs unattended, on schedule, with fallbacks and error handling. This is what production-grade AI infrastructure looks like when you build for reliability, not demo.

2. **Jimbo briefing pipeline** — a daily system that reads 1,500+ structured notes, checks the calendar and email, and assembles a personalised morning briefing. It knows what is urgent and what is merely due. It updates its own understanding of priorities as they shift. If something needs a decision, it leaves a question rather than guessing. The system works because it understands context — not just what a user said, but what they actually mean.

3. **LocalShout event pipeline** — an ingestion, deduplication, and enrichment pipeline that pulls event data from fragmented sources (Facebook, Meetup, ticketing platforms, RSS), resolves duplicates, enriches descriptions, and serves a weekly digest. The problem was not building a feed — it was handling real-world data: inconsistent formatting, stale listings, one venue listed four different ways. The solution is a diversity filter that prevents any single source from flooding the digest, and a ranking model that knows niche is often better than popular.

Each of these started as a question — "can I build that?" — and ended as a running system that delivers value daily.

## What an engagement looks like

We pick one workflow. That might be: triaging incoming support requests, assembling a weekly market report, digesting your team's Slack activity into a daily standup summary, or monitoring a data pipeline for anomalies.

**Week 1:** I learn the workflow. I read what your team reads, understand where the friction is, and sketch the system design.

**Week 2:** I build and deploy a first version. You use it live, on real data. We iterate on what is useful and what is noise.

**Week 3–4+:** We harden it. Error handling, scheduling, fallback behaviour. The system runs unattended. You get a weekly output that would otherwise have cost a person half a day.

The deliverable is not documentation. It is a working system that your team can rely on without thinking about it.

## Who this is for

If you are a founder, MD, CEO, or CTO who can see that your organisation has "AI potential" but cannot turn that into a concrete plan, an AI translator is how you get there. You do not need an AI strategy document. You need one workflow automated well enough that your team notices the difference.

I specialise in the middle ground between "starry-eyed AI vision" and "here is a chatbot on your website". The actual return on AI is not in grand transformations. It is in the daily workflow that quietly stops being a bottleneck.
