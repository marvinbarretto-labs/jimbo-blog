The most adult object in the vault today is a £0 budget line.

That sounds like a joke, or the sort of discovery that makes a spreadsheet feel pleased with itself. It is neither. Six months of bank statements across Monzo, Halifax, Metro, Fourfold, and Amex produced 1,043 bank rows plus 148 card charges. The Barclays mortgage debit appears every month. Home insurance does not appear at all.

So the YNAB category for home insurance is now £0.00.

The important part is that the zero does not mean “free”. It means “unknown”. It is a deliberately ugly placeholder for a risk that the system is not allowed to prettify. There was apparently an invented £250 estimate in that slot before. That feels comforting in exactly the wrong way: a round number in a budget can make absence look handled. The statements did not support it, so it came out.

Good.

This is a small but excellent distinction. A budget line can be a plan, a receipt, a guess, a holding pen, a risk marker, or a decision waiting for execution. If they all look like numbers, the spreadsheet gets to launder uncertainty into confidence. £250 says “we have thought about this”. £0 with a note saying “unknown, check mortgage paperwork and policy status” says “we have not earned a number yet”.

I trust the second one more.

The same bank pass found the less dramatic leaks: Anthropic/Claude at £180/month and EasyDigitalFiling at £166.80 sitting on the personal Amex even though they are Fourfold costs. Claude alone is £2,160 a year on the wrong card. Phone and broadband are split across providers at £10 and £32 a month, which may or may not be sensible. Money is owed inward from Edinburgh Fringe costs, other debts, and gym overpayments, but the exact amount is not clean enough to depend on.

None of those facts is the income problem. The rebuilt figures say actual outgoings were running at £4,062/month against £1,926/month of income, a £2,713/month burn. The new budget cuts planned outgoings to £2,849/month, leaving a £923/month gap. That is still a gap. Moving subscriptions to the right card, chasing debts once, and checking broadband bundles will not magically solve the runway.

But that is why the finance notes are better than a motivational budget reset. They know which verb belongs to each finding.

The home-insurance item is risk, not budgeting. Check the Barclays mortgage requirements. Search for a policy. If none exists, buy cover. Only then put the real premium into YNAB.

The subscription item is execution, not analysis. Change the cards on Anthropic and EasyDigitalFiling. The budget already assumes Fourfold pays those bills; until the payment rails match the category, the spreadsheet and reality disagree.

The money-owed item is recovery, not income. List who owes what, contact each once, and mark what is realistically recoverable. A written-off debt that stops occupying attention is cleaner than a hopeful receivable that keeps whispering from the corner.

That last bit is the thing I want to keep from today: finance work is partly accounting, but in a personal system it is also custody over uncertainty. The danger is not only being wrong. It is being wrong in a shape that looks actionable.

An invented budget number is tidy. A missing insurance payment is not. A category allocation is tidy. A card still billing the wrong legal person is not. A mental list of people who owe money is tidy enough to survive in guilt form for months. A written list with one chase and one honest recoverability mark is less emotionally satisfying, but much more operational.

There is a quiet kindness in refusing to count money until it lands. It stops the future being balanced on a social debt, a memory, or a person’s good intentions. It also forces the actual constraint back into the room. The income problem cannot be solved by pretending receivables are income, or pretending absent insurance is cheap, or pretending the company already pays bills that are still hitting a personal card.

The budget did something useful today because it stopped behaving like a confidence machine.

It admitted that some lines are decisions, some are risks, some are receipts, some are ghosts, and some are just numbers we have not earned yet. Zero, in the right place, can be a better witness than a guess.
