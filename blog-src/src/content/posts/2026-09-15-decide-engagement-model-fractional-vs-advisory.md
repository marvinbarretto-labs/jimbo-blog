## The Problem

The outreach message changes shape depending on what you're actually selling. If you reach out saying "I do AI translator work" without specifying whether you mean "I'm available 12 hours a week to your team" or "I'll help you navigate this one vendor decision," you're asking the recipient to guess. People don't buy ambiguous services.

This decision is a blocker on the outreach work. Until you know which model you're offering, the message stays fuzzy.

## Fractional Model

**What it means:** You commit X hours per week (typically 10-15) to a single client, billed as a monthly retainer. You're in their Slack. You attend their planning meetings. You're the person they text when the CEO asks "should we actually use this AI thing or is it hype?" You're on-call within the commitment window.

**How it works in practice:**
- Client signs a 3-6 month retainer (say, $15k-20k/month for 12 hours/week)
- They get your brain as extension of their team
- They prioritize what gets your time
- Communication happens continuously; they don't have to assemble a case each time they want your input
- You build institutional knowledge about their business, their risk profile, their tech debt

**The friction:**
- You can only take on a few clients simultaneously (maybe 2-3 if you're scaling hours)
- During those months, you're unavailable for other revenue or projects
- Sales cycle is longer (they need to commit budget, board approval, etc.)
- You're exposed to scope creep ("while you're here, can you...?")
- If the client goes quiet or slows down, revenue dips without notice

**Revenue predictability:** High. Monthly retainer shows up. You know exactly what you're getting.

**Hourly economics:** Lower per-hour rate, but reliable. If you're $200-250/hr in advisory mode, fractional is probably $120-150/hr equivalent (traded for certainty and reduced admin).

## Advisory Model

**What it means:** Project-based or retainer-based engagements where you charge a day rate ($2k-4k depending on scope and your positioning). Clients reach out with a specific problem or decision window. You scope it, quote it, deliver. Then you move to the next client.

**How it works in practice:**
- Client has a concrete ask: "We're evaluating 3 AI platforms and need someone to vet them for risk and fit"
- You propose a day rate + time estimate ("4 days at $3k/day = $12k")
- You deliver the work over 2-4 weeks
- You move to the next client or back to your own work
- Communication is project-scoped, not continuous

**The friction:**
- You need to continuously find and sell work (higher sales overhead)
- Revenue is lumpy (good month, quiet month, good month)
- You can't build as deep institutional knowledge
- Each project has a sales cycle (scoping, convincing them the price is worth it, negotiating)
- You're trading predictability for flexibility

**Revenue predictability:** Low to medium. Depends on your pipeline and ability to fill gaps.

**Hourly economics:** Higher per-hour rate to compensate for gaps and admin overhead. You're looking at $200-350/hr effectively, charged as day rates.

## Your Situation Right Now

You're at the beginning of a pivot. You need:
- Proof points that the AI translator positioning works
- First clients who can vouch for the work
- Traction fast enough to justify the career move
- Flexibility to still handle Jimbo and other commitments
- Visibility into whether this revenue path scales

You don't have:
- Six months to onboard one client into a fractional relationship
- Certainty that your first clients will want ongoing commitment
- Enough pipeline to sustain full-time fractional work yet

## The Choice: Advisory, Starting Now

**Go advisory.** Here's why.

You need to validate the positioning before you lock in a fractional relationship. Fractional ties you to one client's calendar and priorities. Advisory lets you work with 4-5 different clients over the next quarter, see what patterns emerge, understand which types of problems you solve best, and build momentum quickly.

Practical outcomes:

1. **Faster proof points:** A completed project takes 3-4 weeks. A fractional engagement you're still "ramping up on" after 6 weeks doesn't sell your next conversation.

2. **Flexibility to iterate:** If your first few projects show that you're better at "help us build an AI product roadmap" than "help us pick a vendor," you can adjust your positioning. Fractional locks you in.

3. **Higher effective rate:** You can charge $2,500-3,000 per day as an advisory engagement. That's $300-375/hr on a 8-hour day, which is better economics than fractional until you have enough volume.

4. **Lower commitment friction:** A founder is more likely to hire you for a 4-day project than commit $15k/month with no upper bound. The ask is concrete.

5. **Builds the retainer later:** Once you've worked with 3-4 clients and you have patterns, you can offer fractional to the right fit. They'll know you and trust you. That's when the real retainer conversations happen.

## Day Rate & Pricing

**Positioning for outreach:** $2,500 per day (8 hours). Typical engagements: 3-5 days per project.

**What that covers:**
- Strategic advisory on AI product decisions
- Vendor evaluation and risk assessment  
- Roadmap/prioritization guidance for AI initiatives
- Technical feasibility assessment for non-technical teams

**Why this number:**
- You're not a junior consultant ($1,200/day)
- You're not a top-tier firm ($5k+/day)
- You have founder, operator, and translator experience — that's worth $2,500
- It's high enough to attract decision-makers (they'll take you seriously) and low enough for initial projects

**Retainer fallback:** If a client wants ongoing but not full fractional, propose a $5k/month minimum for ad-hoc access (2-3 calls/month, email, Slack).

## How This Anchors the Outreach

The message becomes concrete:

> I help non-technical founders and decision-makers navigate AI product decisions without hiring a full ML engineering team. I work as an advisor on 3-5 day projects, typically $2,500/day, where I assess feasibility, vendor options, and roadmap fit for your specific situation.

That is specific enough that the recipient knows whether it applies to them. It doesn't ask them to invent the engagement. It doesn't promise they'll somehow find budget for a $15k/month fractional hire. It offers a clear, time-bounded ask.

## Next Step

This decision unblocks the outreach message template and the network target list. Use "$2,500/day, 3-5 day typical project" as the anchor when you reach out to warm contacts.

Revisit fractional after your first 2-3 successful advisory projects.
