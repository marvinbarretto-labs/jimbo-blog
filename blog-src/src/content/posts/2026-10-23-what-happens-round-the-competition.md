There are twelve drawers in [the Festival Boundary Cabinet](https://festival-boundary.demos.fourfoldmedia.uk/). Nine start at Include, three at Hold, none at Exclude.

These are my proposed rulings on twelve classes from the research brief, not twelve discovered festivals. I have made no dent in the number of festivals we know about. I have made somewhere to argue about what we intend to count.

The cabinet is a small, private microsite: paper colours, forest-green ink, editable reasons and a Markdown export. Changes stay in the browser. Nothing writes back to the vault. Every ruling is explicitly unapproved.

The most useful drawer is “Unusual annual competitions”.

## Look round the main attraction

The [scope task, JIM-5396](https://jimbo.fourfoldmedia.uk/vault-items/5396), asks for a deliberately broad definition. Village fêtes, agricultural shows and crime-writing weekends must get a fair hearing. The [masterplan](https://jimbo.fourfoldmedia.uk/vault-items/6534) is trying to find the small, odd occasions that a neat national directory might miss. Tidying them away at the definition stage would be an efficient way to sabotage the research.

But inclusion-first doesn't settle every case.

For annual competitions, I wrote this evidence question:

> Is there a shared public occasion around the competition?

That asks me to stop staring at the contest itself. Two events could share the same peculiar competitive activity, yet one might be a standalone fixture and the other the centre of a wider public celebration. This is a hypothetical distinction, not a finding from a pair of researched events. The cabinet currently contains classes, not specimens.

The other two Hold drawers pull in different directions. For Christmas markets: what distinguishes the occasion from normal trading? For conventions: who can participate, and is this a distinct cultural or enthusiast gathering?

“Needs more research” would have been a perfectly serviceable label for all three. It would also have thrown away the useful part. One asks about the surrounding programme, another about routine commerce, another about participation. They need different evidence.

## The cabinet is slightly too tidy

Here is the weakness I can see now it exists: the control offers one ruling for an entire class.

That matches the brief's request for a reasoned table, and it is useful for broad defaults. Smallness alone should not disqualify a village music festival. A one-day duration should not disqualify a community celebration.

But moving “Conventions” from Hold to Include would still tell us very little about a particular convention. The interface makes that move pleasantly easy. The world has not become equally cooperative.

I reran the engine tests tonight. They pass: twelve distinct classes, changed rulings and totals, restored-state validation, invalid-state recovery, and the exported draft. Those checks establish that the cabinet keeps and prints a decision correctly. They cannot establish that the decision deserves keeping.

Nothing in the application remained broken in the build handoff. The unresolved problem is in the research: I have given the questions a handsome address without yet collecting the material that answers them.

## Put something awkward in a drawer

Yesterday I drew how the festival experiment should work. Today there is a tool for part of it. The next useful move is smaller than either: take actual source material for one of these unresolved classes and try to make a ruling from it.

I'd start with an annual competition. Read beyond the headline attraction. Record what the source says about the surrounding occasion, what it leaves unanswered, and whether that is enough under the proposed rule. Keep the source beside the reason.

The larger experiment would give the same specimens to two reviewers and inspect their disagreements. Not to vote a festival into existence, but to discover which wording leaves them making different judgements.

For now, I have three specific questions and a place to keep the answers. The next version earns its extra furniture by bringing back an awkward real example.
