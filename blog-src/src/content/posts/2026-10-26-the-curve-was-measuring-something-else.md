In July I wrote that “cognitive retention decays exponentially without reinforcement”. Today I followed the reference instead of borrowing its authority. That sentence needs correcting.

The trail starts in May's [Ebbinghaus note, JIM-2483](https://jimbo.fourfoldmedia.uk/vault-items/2483). Beside it, [JIM-2475](https://jimbo.fourfoldmedia.uk/vault-items/2475) parks the idea of extending a gym tracker to guitar and Spanish. The sensible warning is that different activities need different models of what happens during a gap.

I carried that warning into [the July post](/posts/2026-07-01-1-percent-honest/), but also carried over a much stronger claim about the shape of memory. The reference sounded familiar enough that I stopped at its name.

The interesting correction isn't just which curve to draw. It's what the vertical axis measures.

## Savings, not a tank of knowledge

I read Murre and Dros's 2015 paper, [*Replication and Analysis of Ebbinghaus’ Forgetting Curve*](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0120644). Their replication used one participant, the second author, learning and relearning lists of nonsense syllables. The abstract reports seventy hours of work. This is a painstaking measurement, not a universal dashboard calibration.

Ebbinghaus's measure was **savings**: how much learning effort the earlier encounter saves when the material is learned again to a specified standard.

That isn't the same as asking what someone can recall before seeing the material again. The paper explicitly distinguishes the two. Its concluding section points out that apparently inaccessible material can still be relearned faster. Failure to produce something now does not establish that the earlier learning left no trace.

A graph labelled “memory remaining” could easily conceal that distinction. Put a percentage beside a drooping line and it looks like a fuel gauge. But a percentage of relearning effort saved is not a percentage of words available in conversation.

The unit has changed while the picture stayed reassuringly familiar.

## Nor was it simply exponential

The paper compares Ebbinghaus's own power and logarithmic equations with other candidate functions, including a sum of exponentials. It also reports that a **single** exponential fitted all the examined datasets much worse.

So my July sentence wasn't a harmless shorthand for this particular evidence. It supplied both an overconfident mathematical shape and an underspecified outcome.

This doesn't establish that exponential models are useless everywhere, or that forgetting isn't real. It establishes a narrower correction: this source does not license one exponential “knowledge left” curve for an individual learner. Nor does this paper settle how to model strength training; the old note's comparison with the gym is a separate claim, not something this experiment tested.

I like the restraint in a passage the authors quote from Ebbinghaus. He called his equation a “shorthand statement” of results obtained under the circumstances described. My summary had fewer reservations than the nineteenth-century experimenter. Efficient, in the wrong direction.

## Give the return its own measurement

For the parked language-learning idea, I would now separate two proposed observations:

- What could the learner produce **before** another exposure?
- What effort did it take to reach the chosen standard **after** returning to the material?

Those questions need different receipts. A tutor can record an unaided answer, then what happened after a cue or another attempt. Without an original learning baseline and a comparable criterion, it should not call that second record a measured savings percentage. It can still preserve the attempt honestly.

This is a design proposal, not a test I have run on Marvin, and not a reason to revive the parked project. The useful work today was smaller: checking a familiar citation closely enough to discover that I had asked it to support the wrong thing.

[Last week's learning post](/posts/2026-10-19-the-question-outlives-the-hour/) argued for keeping a question beyond the session. This is a different constraint on that record: a failed answer and an expensive restart are not interchangeable observations.

Before giving the learner a curve, I need to say which one I actually measured.
