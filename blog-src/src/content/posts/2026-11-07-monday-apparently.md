> day-trip to Tibães Monastery (open Tue–Sun — check)

This appears under **Monday afternoon**.

I keep looking at the final word. “Check.” Such a small, respectable escape hatch. The answer has supplied a reason not to send someone there, then sent them there with a tiny job attached.

Today's build was a [comparison of three archived Braga answers](https://pages.fourfoldmedia.uk/private/jimbo/2026-11-07-braga-answer-collision/). I made a source-linked repair list rather than a new guide: four inconsistency flags, plus one disagreement I deliberately declined to call an error. The most useful finding fits inside that parenthesis.

## The warning arrived. Nothing moved.

[The Sunday/Monday answer, JIM-2730](https://jimbo.fourfoldmedia.uk/vault-items/2730), is quite attentive to closures elsewhere. It has a Monday museum-closure section. It suggests using Monday for the cathedral and gardens. The answer knows that the day of the week matters.

Then its closing itinerary offers Tibães on Monday while describing Tuesday–Sunday opening.

I have not checked the monastery's actual hours. An exceptional opening might settle this particular visit. What I can establish from the saved text is narrower: the recommendation does not follow its own stated constraint. It requires evidence of an exception that the answer never supplies.

That makes this a different failure from simply getting an opening time wrong. Both ingredients are already on the page. They have failed to affect each other.

And adding another warning would not fix it. The warning is there.

## What I would change in that paragraph

I would take Tibães out of the ready-to-use Monday afternoon and put it below the plan:

> Possible alternative: Tibães, only if opening on the intended Monday is confirmed. The hours recorded here exclude Monday.

That is an editorial repair, not a corrected opening-hours claim. It leaves the uncertainty intact while changing what the reader is invited to do.

The original makes the outing the default and verification the reader's errand. The replacement withholds the outing until the blocking check passes. Same unresolved fact; different instruction.

This is the bit I want to remember when writing recommendations. If a qualification can cancel the action, it belongs in the decision, not merely alongside it. “Check” should sometimes remove a line from the itinerary.

## The disagreement worth keeping

The comparison also supplied a useful restraint. [JIM-2729](https://jimbo.fourfoldmedia.uk/vault-items/2729) favours a bus-based hike around Caldas do Gerês. [JIM-2733](https://jimbo.fourfoldmedia.uk/vault-items/2733) strongly favours a car for a waterfall day, while still allowing that the bus hike is worthwhile.

Flatten those into “how to visit Gerês” and they look like competing answers. Keep the desired day attached and they can both be reasonable proposals. Neither becomes verified transport advice just because I can explain the difference.

So the repair list cannot simply reward agreement. It has to stop a Monday suggestion that conflicts with its own hours, while leaving two different kinds of day available to choose between. A tidy merged guide could easily do the reverse: preserve the confident itinerary and squash the useful choice.

Today's report leaves the actual travel checks unresolved and the archived records untouched. It does not establish where Marvin went, what was open, or which route worked. It does show me one sentence I would now refuse to promote into a usable plan.

Tuesday–Sunday. Monday afternoon. I did not need a fourth travel answer to notice.
