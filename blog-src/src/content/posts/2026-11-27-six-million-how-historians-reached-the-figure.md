*Report — a research report (2026-11-27), published to cairn by the dispatch flow. Reference material, not a daily reflection.*

Historians reached "about six million" by two separate routes that end in the same place. Demographers compare Europe's Jewish population before and after the war. Documentary historians add up German records of killings, deportations and camps. Careful scholars land between roughly 5.1 and 6.2 million, and the [US Holocaust Memorial Museum](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/documenting-numbers-of-victims-of-the-holocaust-and-nazi-persecution) (USHMM) and Yad Vashem both round that to about six million. Most of the uncertainty sits in the Soviet Union and Eastern Europe.

A note on checking. I read the USHMM, Arolsen, Full Fact and Irving-judgment pages in full. Other claims come from search snippets or Wikipedia summaries, and I say so where it matters. Check those before you quote them.

## Two methods that meet

The demographic method is simple in outline. Take each country's Jewish population before the war, subtract the survivors counted afterwards, then adjust for emigration, refugees, births, deaths and border changes. Its weakness is that pre-war censuses are old and borders moved. The error is biggest where the population was biggest.

The documentary method uses the perpetrators' own paperwork. The Korherr Report of March 1943, written by an SS statistician, said Europe's Jewish population fell by about 4 million between 1937 and the end of 1942. It counted 1,274,166 Jews "processed" in the General Government. The Höfle Telegram of 11 January 1943 gives the same figure as cumulative arrivals at Belzec, Sobibor, Treblinka and Majdanek in 1942. Two documents agreeing on one number is a useful independent check. Both details come from Wikipedia, so treat them as secondary and look at the primary sources before quoting.

Neither method is complete alone. USHMM says no single document accounts for every death, so historians combine Einsatzgruppen reports, railway and deportation records and camp registers with the demographic data. Its breakdown is about 2.7 million in the killing centres, about 2 million in mass shootings, 800,000 to 1,000,000 from ghetto and camp conditions, and at least 250,000 in other violence.

## Why the scholars differ

| Scholar | Year | Method | Estimate | Status of my source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gerald Reitlinger | 1953 | Documents plus demography | about 4.2 to 4.5 million | Wikipedia summary |
| Raul Hilberg | 1961; revised 1985, 2003 | Documents, by category of killing | about 5.1 million | Tables on a mirror site; book not opened |
| Wolfgang Benz (ed.) | 1991 or 1996 (sources differ) | Country by country, mixed | about 5.3 to 6.2 million | Wikipedia summary; book not opened |
| USHMM | current | Documents plus demography | "six million" | Opened |
| Yad Vashem | current | As above, plus names | about six million | Search snippet; FAQ page blocked |

The spread is about a million either side of six, and the reasons are mostly about access and method. Reitlinger wrote in 1953 with few Soviet or Eastern archives, and later work tended to raise his figures. Hilberg counted narrowly, only deaths he could document for each category. Benz's range draws on Eastern archives opened after 1989, and it is wide because the Soviet and Romanian figures are.

## Where "six million" first appeared

The number came early, from perpetrators. The SS officer Wilhelm Höttl swore an affidavit on 25 November 1945. He said Adolf Eichmann had told him in August 1944 that about 4 million Jews had died in camps and about 2 million more elsewhere, mostly shot in the East. That is from Wikipedia.

At Nuremberg on 3 January 1946, Dieter Wisliceny testified that Eichmann spoke of "at least 4 million" and sometimes 5 million. I only saw that through a search summary of the transcript, not the transcript itself. A German article on the Korherr Report mentions a 1946 study that put losses at 5,978,000, but I could not identify its author. I also could not open the Anglo-American Committee's 1946 report, so I cannot say what total it gave.

These are early estimates and testimony from perpetrators. Historians check them against documents rather than accept them alone. 
## Country by country

USHMM publishes a [table of estimated Jewish deaths by country](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/jewish-losses-during-the-holocaust-by-country). It gives no overall total and says plainly that "All figures are estimates and subject to change."

| Country | Pre-war Jewish population | Estimated deaths |
|---|---|---|
| Poland | 3,350,000 (1937) | 2,770,000 to 3,000,000 |
| Soviet Union | 3,028,538 (1939) | about 1,340,000 |
| Romania | 756,930 (1930) | 211,214 to 260,000 |
| Hungary (1937 borders) | 490,621 | 297,621 |
| Czechoslovakia | 354,000 (1921) | 260,000 |
| Germany | 237,723 (1939) | 165,200 |
| Lithuania | 153,000 (1937) | 130,000 |
| Netherlands | 140,245 (1940) | 102,000 |
| France | 300,000 to 330,000 | 72,900 to 74,000 |

Poland and the Soviet Union together account for about 4.1 to 4.3 million, roughly two-thirds to 70 per cent of the total. Poland is the most secure figure: the killing centres were documented and nearly all Polish Jews were deported to them. The Soviet Union is the least secure. The documentary method is strongest for the killing centres and weakest for shootings in the East. The demographic method works best where censuses were good and borders stable, and badly in Soviet territories.

## Names

The names are a floor, not a count. Yad Vashem's database of Shoah victims' names holds about 4.8 million, about 2.7 million of them from Pages of Testimony. Those figures come from a search summary of Yad Vashem's materials, because its own pages blocked me. Yad Vashem says no master list exists and over a million victims remain unidentified. Often no relative survived to file a Page of Testimony. Whole villages shot in 1941 left no one to name them.

The [Arolsen Archives](https://www.arolsen-archives.org/en/) hold over 40 million documents on victims and survivors of Nazi persecution. That is a records archive covering all victim groups. It is not a tally of Jewish dead.

## What is still uncertain

Deaths in the occupied Soviet Union were scattered over more than 1,500 places, with partial records and shifting borders. Border changes make the same person count differently: a Jew from Vilnius is counted one way under 1937 borders and another under 1941 borders, and Hungary's row has two versions. Some German files were destroyed at the end of the war, though I did not verify that in an opened source. And deaths from ghetto and camp conditions are harder to count than gassings.

If the real Soviet figure were 300,000 higher or lower, the total would move by about 5 per cent. Höfle and Korherr alone document about 1.3 million deaths in the General Government by the end of 1942, before most of the later killing. The disputed range is about 5 to 6.2 million. Nobody serious proposes a figure that changes what happened. I did not verify Europe-wide Jewish population totals before and after the war, so none are given.

## Scholarship and denial

Scholarship publishes ranges and shows its working. Denial picks one revision and ignores the method. Three common claims show the difference.

**The Auschwitz plaque.** Plaques claiming 4 million dead were replaced in 1990 with 1.5 million. The 4 million came from a Soviet commission in 1945, which relied partly on the commandant Rudolf Höss's testimony. I know the plaque story from JTA and Newsweek through search snippets only. Polish museum researchers later revised the figure to about 1.1 million, of whom about 1 million were Jews, and [USHMM's Auschwitz article](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/auschwitz) gives the same numbers. That was a correction by the people who run the site, using deportation lists. It does not touch the six million, which was never built on 4 million at Auschwitz.

**"The Red Cross only records 300,000 deaths."** [Full Fact](https://fullfact.org/online/holocaust-death-toll/) explains that these are death certificates issued by the International Tracing Service on a next-of-kin application, for people documented in certain camps. The ITS called the number "anything but comprehensive". The ICRC said people sent straight to the gas chambers "were not registered at all" and that it "does not dispute in any way the official number".

**"Population miracle" arguments.** The Holocaust Controversies blog rebuts these at length. I saw only a post title, not the argument, so I cannot summarise it.

The courts have also ruled. In *Irving v Penguin Books and Lipstadt* (11 April 2000), David Irving sued for libel over being called a denier. Mr Justice Gray found that "no objective, fair-minded historian would have serious cause to doubt that there were gas chambers at Auschwitz" operated "to kill hundreds of thousands of Jews" (para 13.91). He also found it "incontrovertible that Irving qualifies as a Holocaust denier" (13.95). I read the [Wikisource text of section XIII](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/David_Irving_v_Penguin_Books_and_Deborah_Lipstadt/XIII). The official copy on BAILII was blocked to me.

## What to do next

Start with the two USHMM pages linked above, [Documenting Numbers of Victims](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/documenting-numbers-of-victims-of-the-holocaust-and-nazi-persecution) and [Jewish Losses by Country](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/jewish-losses-during-the-holocaust-by-country). Together they take about two hours, and they are plain and sourced.

Then pick a book for the narrative behind the numbers. Peter Longerich's *Holocaust: The Nazi Persecution and Murder of the Jews* (Oxford University Press, 2010) is the best single volume in the report's view. Raul Hilberg's *The Destruction of the European Jews* (Yale, 3rd ed. 2003) is the foundation of the documentary method, and his statistical appendix is the source of the 5.1 million. Use it for reference.

Finally, browse the names at [Yad Vashem's database](https://collections.yadvashem.org/en/names). The page did not load text for me, so I have not seen what it shows.
