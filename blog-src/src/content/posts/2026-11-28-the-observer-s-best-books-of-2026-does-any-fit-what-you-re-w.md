## Summary

One match is worth your time: **Carissa Véliz, *Prophecy***, a book about who gets to make predictions about people, and what those predictions then do to them. **I couldn't check the whole list.** The Observer's site refuses Claude's search and fetch tools, and I didn't try to get round that. The email gives only the link, so this is not a verdict on every book on it.

- **The other non-fiction, the fiction, the poetry and the graphic novels:** my searches found no other title I could tie to this list.

## Why Prophecy fits

- **Scored predictions about you.** You have an idea on hold (JIM-7637) for coaches to predict what you'll do and be scored on it from your data. Its hold says it waits for the coach experiment's stop-or-go verdict, due from 2 December [5][6].
- **Your 17 November reading.** After you watched *Minority Report*, Claude (Opus 5.5) added to your lists *Weapons of Math Destruction*, the original Dick story, *Devs* and *Person of Interest*, among others. These were its recommendations; the saved notes don't show that you've read or watched them [7].
- **What the book argues.** Descriptions and reviews say it treats prediction as often more about power than knowledge, says predictions about people tend to fulfil themselves, and covers algorithms that affect loans, jobs, housing and transplants [8][9][10]. In a related essay (not confirmed as book text), Véliz says the easiest way to predict behaviour is to influence it [11].

**The limit of the match:** the examples I saw are mostly institutions judging people. I found nothing showing that the book covers self-tracking, or predictions you ask for about yourself. Applying it to your coaches is my analogy. I haven't read the book.

## The author

- Associate professor at Oxford's Faculty of Philosophy and Institute for Ethics in AI, and a fellow of Hertford College [8].
- Wrote *Privacy Is Power*; also wrote or edited academic books on privacy and digital ethics [8][10].
- **Interests to bear in mind:** Véliz sits on the board of the Proton Foundation, Proton's main shareholder. Proton sells privacy products and an AI assistant [17][18]. Véliz also offers paid-circuit keynotes through speaking agencies, including on forecasting [19]. Neither makes the argument wrong, but both are close to its subject.

## Recommendation

1. **Try it free first:** read the LitHub book excerpt [20] and the Aeon essay [11]. The excerpt is mainly history; the essay is the closer test of whether the argument is useful to you.
2. **If the essay lands, read the book before you restart JIM-7637.** Expect the loose structure that two reviewers noted. The UK edition is Swift Press: hardback out 7 May 2026 (ISBN 9781800757240), paperback due 25 March 2027. I didn't find a UK price [9].
3. **One design question to take to the coaches** (my inference, not the book's advice). If you see a prediction before it resolves, it can change what you do. The score then measures the forecast and its effect on you together. JIM-7637 shows predictions that clash with your week plan beside the plan. If you also want to know how well a coach knows you, keep some predictions hidden until they resolve and score those separately. With only a few predictions, that comparison will tell you little at first.
4. **To check the rest of the list,** open the article from the 27 November email and paste its text into a chat. Then every title can be matched properly. Part two of the guide is still to come.
